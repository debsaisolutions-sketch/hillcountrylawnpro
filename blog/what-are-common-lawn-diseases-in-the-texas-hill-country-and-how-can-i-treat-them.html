What Are Common Lawn Diseases in the Texas Hill Country and How Can I Treat Them?

Maintaining a lush, green lawn in the Texas Hill Country can be a challenge, especially when lawn diseases strike. Understanding the signs of these diseases and knowing how to treat them promptly can keep your lawn healthy and vibrant.

Common Lawn Diseases in the Texas Hill Country

Brown Patch

Brown patch is a fungal disease that thrives in warm, humid conditions. It appears as circular brown patches on your lawn, and if left untreated, it can spread rapidly.

Take-All Patch

This disease affects St. Augustine grass, which is common in the Hill Country. You'll notice yellowing grass that eventually dies off, creating patches in your lawn.

Dollar Spot

Dollar spot is characterized by small, silver-dollar-sized patches of bleached grass. It thrives in low-nitrogen soil and can spread if not treated.

How to Treat Lawn Diseases

DIY Treatments

  • Improve Air Circulation: Trim back overgrown shrubs and trees to improve sunlight and air circulation.
  • Water Wisely: Water your lawn deeply but infrequently to prevent moisture-loving fungi from taking hold.
  • Fertilize Appropriately: Use a balanced fertilizer to strengthen your grass and make it less susceptible to diseases.

When to Call a Local Pro

If the disease persists despite your efforts, it might be time to call a local lawn care professional. They can provide targeted treatments, such as fungicides, and offer advice specific to the unique climate of the Texas Hill Country.

Preventing Future Lawn Issues

Regular lawn maintenance is key to preventing diseases. Mow your lawn at the recommended height for your grass type, aerate annually, and keep an eye out for early signs of trouble.
